## Theme 1 — Clarity-first frameworks

### BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front)

**Framework:** US Army Regulation AR 25-50, *Preparing and Managing Correspondence*
(2013). Official doctrine requiring that the main point appear in the first sentence
or paragraph of any official communication, with supporting detail following.
**Prescription:** State the conclusion, recommendation, or headline finding first.
Never open with context, background, or methodology when the reader needs the
verdict.
**Source:** US Army Publishing Directorate.
https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/pdf/web/r25_50.pdf

---

### Inverted Pyramid

**Framework:** Journalism standard codified by the Poynter Institute and taught
across professional writing programs. Originated in wire-service constraints;
validated by digital reading-behavior research showing readers stop early.
**Prescription:** Lead with the most newsworthy / decision-relevant information.
Supporting context, methodology, and background descend in order of decreasing
importance. Readers who leave early still get the key point.
**Source:** Poynter Institute.
https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2003/the-inverted-pyramid/

---

### Minto Pyramid Principle / SCQA

**Framework:** Barbara Minto, *The Pyramid Principle: Logic in Writing and Thinking*
(Prentice Hall, 1987; McKinsey & Company training standard). The SCQA structure
(Situation → Complication → Question → Answer) ensures every document answers the
reader's question before defending the answer. *(partial — primary text paywalled;
prescriptions drawn from McKinsey published training digests and verified secondary
sources.)*
**Prescription:** Answer before argument. Group supporting ideas under the answer;
never require the reader to infer the point from accumulated evidence.
**Source (secondary digest):** McKinsey & Company "The Pyramid Principle in business
writing." https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/mckinsey-design/how-we-help-clients

Related: Minto B. *The Pyramid Principle.* Pearson Education, 2009 edition.
https://www.pearson.com/us/higher-education/product/Minto-Pyramid-Principle-The/9780273659051.html

---

### Progressive Disclosure

**Framework:** Nielsen Norman Group — research-backed UX principle. Information is
presented at the level of detail the user currently needs; deeper layers are
available on demand.
**Prescription:** Headline by default. Reasoning, traces, and raw data behind a
"see more" or a clearly subordinated section. Don't make readers scroll past
supporting data to reach the finding.
**Source:** Nielsen Norman Group.
https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/

Related: Microsoft HAX Guideline G11 — "Make clear what the system can and cannot
do; scope by default, full detail on demand."
https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/

---

### Up-Goer Five / Plain Language Threshold

**Framework:** Randall Munroe, *Thing Explainer* (2015), and the "Up-Goer Five"
XKCD illustration (#1133). Popular instantiation of the principle that any concept
can be explained in plain language; technical vocabulary is a choice, not a
necessity. Not a formal standard — cited here as a sanity-check heuristic.
**Prescription:** Could you explain this to a smart non-specialist in one sentence
using common words? If not, the concept is probably underexplained, not inherently
complex.
**Source:** https://xkcd.com/1133/

---

### Gricean Maxims

**Framework:** H. P. Grice, "Logic and Conversation," *Syntax and Semantics* Vol. 3
(Academic Press, 1975). Four maxims governing cooperative communication: Quantity
(say as much as needed, no more), Quality (say only what you believe to be true),
Relation (be relevant), Manner (be clear, brief, orderly).
**Prescription:** Every sentence must pass all four. Violations are not just style
failures — they are communication failures that force the reader to infer intent.
*(partial — original behind JSTOR paywall; prescriptions from verified philosophy
and linguistics textbook digests.)*
**Source (digest):** Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Implicature."
https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/implicature/

## Theme 2 — Plain-language standards

### plainlanguage.gov / Plain Writing Act 2010

**Framework:** US Plain Writing Act (Pub. L. 111-274, 2010). Requires federal
agencies to write public documents in plain language. The Federal Plain Language
Guidelines codify the full standard: short sentences, active voice, common words,
logical organization, reader-first structure.
**Prescription:** Use active voice and present tense. Choose common words over
formal equivalents. Write for the reader, not the bureaucracy. One topic per
paragraph, topic sentence first.
**Source:** https://www.plainlanguage.gov/guidelines/

---

### ISO 24495-1:2023 — Plain Language

**Framework:** International Organization for Standardization, *ISO 24495-1:2023
Plain language — Part 1: Governing principles and guidelines*. First international
standard for plain language. Four governing principles: documents must be
**Relevant** (reader's needs first), **Findable** (easy to locate information),
**Understandable** (clear on first reading), **Usable** (enables action). *(partial
— full standard requires purchase; prescriptions drawn from ISO press release,
published summaries, and PLAIN Network digests.)*
**Source (summary):** ISO press release.
https://www.iso.org/standard/78907.html

---

### Hemingway App / Readability Grades

**Framework:** Automated readability tools operationalizing the Flesch-Kincaid Grade
Level formula (Flesch 1948; Kincaid et al. 1975). The Hemingway Editor flags hard
sentences (grade 10+), very hard sentences (grade 14+), passive voice, and adverb
clusters. Grade 8–9 is the target for professional writing; grade 6–8 for broad
audiences.
**Prescription:** Aim for grade 8–9. Flag and rewrite every sentence graded
"hard" or "very hard." Eliminate passive voice unless the actor is unknown or
irrelevant.
**Source:** Hemingway Editor. https://hemingwayapp.com/
Related: Flesch R. "A new readability yardstick." *J Applied Psychology* 32(3):221–233, 1948.
Kincaid JP et al. *Derivation of New Readability Formulas.* Naval Air Station, Memphis, 1975.

---

### Nielsen Norman Group — Scannability and Reading Behavior

**Framework:** Nielsen Norman Group, multiple studies 1997–2023. Key findings:
users read ≤28% of words on a web page on average (Nielsen 1997, confirmed in later
replications); reading follows an F-pattern (first line full, second shorter, then
vertical scan — Nielsen & Pernice 2010); halving copy from 806 to 389 words lifted
usability by 58%, halving again to 111 words pushed further to 124% improvement
(Nielsen 1997).
**Prescription:** Structure must carry the message without requiring full-prose
reading. Use headings with info-bearing words first. Use bullets where content is
genuinely list-shaped. Front-load paragraphs. White space is not decoration.
**Source:** Nielsen J. "How Users Read on the Web." NN/g, 1997.
https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/

F-pattern study: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/

## Theme 3 — Numbers and data communication

### Knaflic — Storytelling with Data

**Framework:** Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic, *Storytelling with Data* (Wiley, 2015).
Canonical practitioner framework for data communication. Key prescriptions: title
every chart with the insight, not the label; maximize data-ink (Edward Tufte's
ratio); remove gridlines, unnecessary color, and decorative elements that carry no
information.
**Prescription (for /distill):** A metric block with no prose interpretation is a
number dump. Every chart title must state a finding ("Conversion dropped 12 pp after
the Nov 3 deploy"), not a description ("Conversion rate by date"). Every number
needs an explicit interpretation sentence.
**Source:** Knaflic CN. *Storytelling with Data.* Wiley, 2015.
https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/books

---

### Tufte — The Visual Display of Quantitative Information

**Framework:** Edward R. Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*
(Graphics Press, 1983; 2nd ed. 2001). Foundational work establishing the data-ink
ratio (maximize ink devoted to data, minimize non-data ink), the concept of
chartjunk (decorative elements that obscure rather than inform), and the lie factor
(visual distortion relative to the data). *(partial — physical book; prescriptions
from verified published summaries and Tufte's own web materials.)*
**Prescription:** Strip every chart element that does not encode data. The
data-ink ratio should approach 1. Never use 3D charts, gratuitous color gradients,
or decorative icons on data.
**Source:** Tufte ER. *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information.* Graphics
Press, 2001. https://www.edwardtufte.com/tufte/books_vdqi

---

### Stray / Tow Center — Numbers in Journalism

**Framework:** Jonathan Stray, *The Curious Journalist's Guide to Data* (Tow
Center for Digital Journalism, Columbia University, 2016). Research-backed guide on
how numbers mislead when context is absent: every number needs a comparison to mean
something; absolute and relative changes must both be reported; denominators matter.
**Prescription:** Attach context to every number: unit, denominator/sample size,
baseline or comparison, direction, one-line interpretation. Report absolute
alongside relative change. "Up 30%" means nothing without knowing from what base.
**Source:** Stray J. *The Curious Journalist's Guide to Data.* Tow Center for
Digital Journalism, 2016.
https://towcenter.gitbooks.io/curious-journalist-s-guide-to-data/

Related: Sense About Science. *Making Sense of Statistics* (2010).
https://senseaboutscience.org/activities/making-sense-of-statistics/

## Theme 4 — Technical to executive communication

### Google Technical Writing One

**Framework:** Google Developer Documentation Style Guide and Technical Writing
courses (public). Covers active voice, present tense, short sentences, defining
terms on first use, the "So what?" test for every claim, and task-oriented
structure.
**Prescription:** Define every technical term at first use. Write in active voice
and present tense. Apply the "So what?" test: if a sentence doesn't help the reader
decide or act, cut or rewrite it.
**Source:** Google Technical Writing Courses.
https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one

Google Developer Style Guide: https://developers.google.com/style

---

### Strunk & White / Zinsser

**Framework:** William Strunk Jr. & E. B. White, *The Elements of Style* (4th ed.,
Pearson, 1999) — "Omit needless words" (Rule 17); "Prefer the specific to the
general, the definite to the vague, the concrete to the abstract." William Zinsser,
*On Writing Well* (7th ed., HarperCollins, 2006) — "The secret of good writing is
to strip every sentence to its cleanest components."
**Prescription:** Every word must do work. Cut filler phrases, throat-clearing, and
redundant qualifiers. Re-read every sentence asking: what does this add? If the
answer is "nothing," cut it.
**Source:** Strunk W, White EB. *The Elements of Style.* 4th ed. Pearson, 1999.
Zinsser W. *On Writing Well.* 7th ed. HarperCollins, 2006.

---

### USC / Purdue — Executive Summary Checklist

**Framework:** USC Dornsife Center for Excellence in Research and Purdue OWL
(Online Writing Lab) executive-summary guidelines. Prescriptions: executive
summaries must be self-contained (a stranger reads them without the underlying
document), ≤10% of the source document's length, lead with the conclusion, and
state the recommendation or finding explicitly.
**Prescription:** Any summary must stand alone. Include the conclusion and
recommendation in the first paragraph. Never assume the reader will read the full
document.
**Source:** Purdue OWL: https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/common_writing_assignments/executive_summaries.html
USC Research Guides: https://libguides.usc.edu/writingguide/executivesummary

## Theme 5 — AI-specific legibility and trust

### Microsoft HAX Guidelines

**Framework:** Microsoft Research, *Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction* (2019; HAX
Toolkit 2020+). 18 evidence-backed guidelines for AI system design. Relevant to
output clarity:
- **G2 (Set appropriate expectations):** Match language and confidence to what the
  system actually knows. Don't overstate. Categorical confidence (High/Med/Low)
  outperforms numeric where the underlying estimate is rough.
- **G11 (Make clear what the system can and cannot do; scope + detail on demand):**
  Default to headline-level output; detail available on request.
**Prescription:** AI output must communicate its own confidence calibration honestly.
Users who can't tell when an AI is uncertain will over-trust it.
**Source:** Amershi S et al. "Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction." *CHI 2019.*
https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/

HAX Toolkit: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/

---

### Google PAIR Guidebook

**Framework:** Google People + AI Research (PAIR), *People + AI Guidebook* (2019).
Design guidance for AI-powered products. Relevant prescriptions: calibrate user
trust to actual system reliability; prefer categorical over numeric confidence
expressions when precision is not justified; explain the basis for AI suggestions;
make failure modes visible rather than hiding them behind softened language.
**Prescription:** Calibrated language — "I'm not certain" is better than a 63%
confidence score that implies false precision. Declare uncertainty rather than
obscuring it.
**Source:** Google PAIR Guidebook.
https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook/

---

### Anthropic — Output quality principles

**Framework:** Anthropic internal and public guidance on Claude output quality.
Relevant prescriptions: be direct and confident; specify output format explicitly;
ground factual claims in sources; avoid tiny-bullet strings that fragment reasoning;
faithfulness to source material is the foundation of trust.
**Prescription:** State the finding directly. Format output for the reader's task,
not the model's convenience. Never present a claim stronger than what the source
supports.
**Source:** Anthropic model card and usage documentation.
https://www.anthropic.com/research

---

### OpenAI Prover-Verifier Games — Legibility must be trained for

**Framework:** Kirchner et al., "Prover-Verifier Games Improve Legibility of LLM
Outputs." arXiv:2407.13692 (2024). Finding: AI models trained only on correctness
produce outputs that are correct but illegible to non-expert verifiers. Legibility
— the property of being understandable and auditable by a human who can't
independently verify the answer — requires explicit training signal. Overconfident
framing erodes trust; models default to legibility-degrading behaviors without
constraint.
**Prescription:** Correct output is not sufficient. Output must also be legible —
structured so a non-expert can follow the reasoning and check it. This requires
deliberate effort, not just accuracy optimization.
**Source:** Kirchner JH et al. "Prover-Verifier Games Improve Legibility of LLM
Outputs." arXiv:2407.13692, 2024.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13692

### SummEval — Summarization quality dimensions

**Framework:** Fabbri AR et al., "SummEval: Re-evaluating Summarization Evaluation."
*Transactions of the Association for Computational Linguistics* 9:391–409, 2021.
Meta-evaluation establishing four summarization quality dimensions: **Coherence**
(logical flow), **Consistency / Faithfulness** (not inventing or distorting facts
from the source), **Fluency** (grammatical correctness), **Relevance** (covering
what matters). Faithfulness emerged as the top quality dimension — a fluent,
coherent summary that misrepresents the source is worse than a clunky faithful one.
**Prescription:** Faithfulness to the source is non-negotiable. Every simplification
must preserve the original's confidence level, scope, and caveats.
**Source:** Fabbri AR et al. "SummEval." *TACL* 9:391–409, 2021.
https://aclanthology.org/2021.tacl-1.24/

---

### G-Eval — LLM-as-judge evaluation

**Framework:** Liu Y et al., "G-Eval: NLG Evaluation using GPT-4 with Better Human
Alignment." arXiv:2303.16634 (2023). Framework for using LLMs as evaluators of
natural-language generation quality, operationalizing the SummEval dimensions with
chain-of-thought scoring rubrics. Confirms faithfulness as the dimension most
correlated with human trust judgments.
**Prescription:** When evaluating a summary or distilled output for quality, assess
faithfulness first. Fluency and coherence are secondary.
**Source:** Liu Y et al. "G-Eval." arXiv:2303.16634, 2023.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16634
